# Case 01 — The Echo at Hollin · Treatment

**Style:** 90s Apocalypse Anime (`styles/apocalypse-anime-90s/STYLE.md` in this repo)
**Brief (defaults, user delegated):** an original story · English narrator + burned-in captions + `ep01.srt` · 120 s · 1920×1080 at 24 fps · no storyboard stop · YouTube scary-sci-fi explainer channel · no LemoLab credit.
**Written before opening the base demo's DEMO.md**, as AGENTS.md requires.

---

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **The countdown of files.** Each decoded fragment arrives closer to "now" (12 days → 3 days → 9 hours → tonight). The narrator explains the case like an investigator; the timestamps become the clock of the film. *Strength:* built-in escalation, and it fits the Intertitle Barrage perfectly.
2. **One night, three times.** The same night at the array told three times: as the crew lived it, as the files described it, and as the investigators reconstructed it. *Strength:* eerie repetition. *Weakness:* at 2 minutes the third pass gets rushed, and repetition needs time to land.
3. **The interview tape.** A found debriefing tape of the surviving technician, intercut with the station. *Weakness:* a survivor kills the ending ("never found"), and a talking head fights the style's stillness.

**Choice: 1.** It gives one subject (the night crew), one goal (understand the call and survive it), one turn (the reply is sent) and a self-explaining clock, which an explainer needs. The opening and ending come from the story itself: the station under power lines on an ordinary summer evening, and the same frame at night, with the dish now raised toward the sky.

## 2. Logline and arc

**Logline.** In August 1999 a rural radio array receives a distress call from a dead probe, spoken in the night crew's own voices and dated in the future. Each new file arrives closer to the present, until one is dated tonight and begs them not to reply.

- **Setup (0–24 s):** the ordinary array, the call, the impossible voices.
- **Escalation (24–60 s):** a prediction comes true (the vending machine); the files close in; Dev's own voice says he is gone.
- **Turn (60–75 s):** protocol forces a vote; two systems against Mara; the array transmits. Cross Light.
- **Reveal (75–97 s):** the timestamps were a countdown of distance; every reply guided it closer; the call was bait in trusted voices.
- **Coda (97–120 s):** never found; the array still transmits nightly in their voices. Final card: DON'T ANSWER.

## 3. Benchmark

- **Primary:** mid-90s apocalyptic TV anime (see the style's `REFERENCES.md`). *Learn:* squeezed-serif cards as chapter punctuation; holds longer than comfortable; city furniture (poles, wires, a vending machine) as characters; command-post monitors as messengers; calm music against horror. *Don't take:* any character, robot, creature, organisation, logo, title wording or musical theme.
- **Secondary:** *Contact* (1997), the signal-discovery sequence. *Learn:* the procedural calm of scientists hearing something impossible; headphones and waveforms as drama. *Don't take:* shots or dialogue.
- **Channel benchmark:** narrated horror explainers in the analog-horror tradition. *Learn:* a flat, documentary narrator; "investigators later found…"; an ending that implicates the listener.

## 6. Cue map

- **Grid:** 60 BPM, 4/4 → 1 beat = 1 s, 1 bar = 4 s, 30 bars. Cuts land on whole or half seconds whenever the story allows.
- **Bars 1 (0–4 s):** no music; four chirps on the cards. Cicadas start at 3.0 on the downbeat.
- **Bars 2–5 (4–21 s):** no music; beds only (cicadas, hum, room tone). The restraint makes the title cue land.
- **Bars 6–11 (21–44 s):** a **string chorale** in D minor, slow half notes, low register, at −20 dB under the VO; a harpsichord-like pluck doubles the top line from bar 9.
- **Bar 12 (44–48 s):** the chorale stops; four sub-thumps on the beats (barrage); silence on the downbeat of bar 13's pickup (47.6).
- **Bars 13–15 (48.4–60 s):** a single sustained cello pedal (D) with a slow tremolo; it gets louder through Dev's line.
- **Bars 16–18 (60–72 s):** relay clicks on beats 1 and 3; the pedal climbs a semitone at 67; the two-tone alarm enters at 69.4 (0.5 s per tone).
- **Bar 18, beat 3 (71 s):** the beam swell; the impact at 72.2 (sub drop and a noise burst); ringing to 73.6; then **digital silence** to 75.6.
- **Bars 19–22 (75.6–90 s):** solo piano, sparse, a descending figure on every second beat; distant choir "ah" pads from 81.
- **Bars 23–24 (90–97 s):** the choir swells to its loudest (still under the VO), in a major key; the irony of beauty over the reveal. It ends on the cut at 97.4.
- **Bars 25–30 (97.4–120 s):** no music. The beds only (cicadas at night become crickets and hum), the signal light's soft tick every 1.5 s; everything stops dead at 120.0.

## 8. Subtitle and title design

- **Captions:** Archivo Narrow 600, 50 px, white with a 4 px #0b0b0b outline, centred at the bottom, 90 px above the frame edge; two lines at most. Playback lines are set in italics with a small tag: `MARA — PLAYBACK`, in signal orange IBM Plex Mono, 22 px above the line. Exported as `ep01.srt`. Holds ≥ max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s), yielding to the next line.
- **Cards:** Noto Serif Display 900, white on black, horizontal scale 0.62 (squeezed). The title card is an L-block: **CASE 01** big and left-aligned, **THE ECHO AT HOLLIN** set vertically down the right edge at 0.6× size. Barrage cards fill 70–90 % of the frame height and may touch the edges. The final card DON'T ANSWER. is black on white (the one inversion of the card scheme), held 2 s.
- **Interface:** Archivo Narrow 700 caps for headers (TRANSMITTING, CREW VOICE MATCH), IBM Plex Mono 500 for numbers; rolling numbers settle in 0.6 s.
- On-screen card texts never repeat a caption at the same moment.
