# 90s Apocalypse Anime — References & analysis

Grammar only. Nothing below is copied into a film: no names, logos, mecha or creature designs, card texts, melodies, the Matisse typeface, or specific shots. The source works are never named on screen.

## Primary reference: *Neon Genesis Evangelion* (TV 1995–96) and *The End of Evangelion* (1997)

| Element | What the reference does | What we learn (rule) | What we never take |
|---|---|---|---|
| Title cards | Black-and-white cards set only in an extra-bold Mincho (Matisse EB), often **mechanically compressed** into interlocking compositions; a hidden homage to 60s–70s Toho film titles. Paired with all-caps Helvetica, and the same pairing spreads into the HUD interfaces [1][2][3] | Type is a picture: one ultra-heavy high-contrast serif, squeezed or stretched to fill a block, laid out in right-angle "L" or stair shapes, white on black. A grotesque sans in caps is the only second voice | the Matisse face itself (commercial, the official EVA release); the show's title texts or episode-title wording |
| Stillness | Long static shots that show a character's inner state from the first episodes on; one shot in ep. 24 holds completely still for **more than 45 s**; compositions are mostly static [4][5] | A held, locked frame with nothing (or one tiny thing) moving is the main tool of dread. Holds of 5–15 s are normal, and one per film may run 20 s or longer | a specific composition (the elevator, the lake) |
| City furniture as character | Telephone poles, buildings, vending machines and public phones were drawn **as cels**, because Anno wanted them treated as characters [6] | Power lines, poles, signals and machines are flat cels with their own line and shadow, not soft background paint. They frame and cut the sky | a specific street or city |
| Tokusatsu lineage | Akio Jissoji (Ultraman, Ultraseven) as a major aesthetic influence: extreme angles, obscured figures, dreamlike collapse; episodes 25–26 borrow from Jissoji's "Nightmare of the Fourth Planet" [7][8]. Trains via Osamu Dezaki [8] | Extreme low and high angles; figures cut by foreground objects; scale shown through architecture; a final act may break the medium (sketches, raw cels, text) | any tokusatsu hero or monster |
| Interfaces | Command-post monitors with danger-first palettes: black ground, orange headers, green nominal data, red alerts; hexagonal or warning-stripe status zones; a three-computer vote [9][10][11] | The interface is a messenger of horror. Alarms take the only red/orange in the frame, a decision can be staged as a split-screen vote, and data rolls on screen | MAGI/NERV names, the logo, the exact panel layouts |
| Editing | Flash intertitles; rapid inserts; montage cut against silence; abrupt cuts to black | A flash card lasts 2–12 frames; a barrage is a sequence of cards cut on a rhythm; silence is cut hard, not faded | — |
| Sound | Cicadas as the bed of a hot, ordinary day; trains; public-domain classical choral and string music set against violence | The ordinary sound bed continues under extraordinary events; a calm classical cue plays against horror; true silence before an impact | any melody from the series' score |

## Secondary references (period siblings)

- *Serial Experiments Lain* (1998): wires hum, shadows carry static noise, and the network is a place. → Electrical hum as a horror bed; dither and noise patterns in shadows (used rarely).
- *Ghost in the Shell* (1995): long, wordless city plates under a choral cue. → One contemplative "plate montage" per film is allowed.
- *Akira* (1988): light as destruction; trails; a white-out. → A cross- or column-shaped light event is the style's shock.

## Visual analysis → numbers we use

| Parameter | Value in the style |
|---|---|
| Frame | 1920×1080 at 24 fps; action stepped on twos (12 fps), held drawings on threes |
| Flash card | 2–12 frames; at least 3 in a barrage; held cards ≥ reading time |
| Long hold | 5–15 s typical; one per film ≥ 20 s |
| Card type | heavy high-contrast serif, horizontal scale 0.55–0.75 (squeezed) or 1.3–1.6 (stretched) |
| Grounds | hospital white `#ecebe4`, night teal `#0e2a30`, black `#060606`, sunset `#e8792b`→`#f2c46b` |
| Alarm | red `#d81f26`, signal orange `#ff7a1a`, nominal green `#38d26a` (UI only) |
| Accent of "the thing" | violet `#5b2a86` + acid green `#9be34a`, never on people |
