# Ad under test: Sprigly · Sunday standoff (directed)

A 20-second vertical video ad (9:16) that appears between other videos in the short-video feed on people's phones. Many people watch with the sound off; every spoken line is also burned in as a caption. People can scroll past at any second.

## The product
Sprigly is a liquid houseplant feed with seaweed extract, dosed with a measuring cap, from a new brand. Feeding your houseplants takes one capful in the watering can every two weeks, with no guessing.
- Contains seaweed extract plus nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium (NPK 3-1-2)
- Dose is one capful (5 ml) per litre of water
- Label directions: feed every two weeks in spring and summer, monthly in autumn and winter
- 250 ml bottle makes 50 litres of feed
- Glass bottle; refill pouches sold separately

The ad only claims: "One capful per litre", "Every two weeks in spring and summer", "With seaweed extract".

## Who it is made for
Adults in flats with a few houseplants who care about them but aren't expert gardeners. The moment: Sunday-morning watering, when they spot a droopy, pale-leaved plant on the windowsill and don't know what it needs. Their problem: They water and hope, unsure how much to feed or how often, and feel quietly guilty when a plant looks tired.

## Look and sound
The picture is drawn like this: layered card-stock paper cut-out, warm cream walls, a teal-and-sand kitchen, soft lamp shadows under every piece, cut edges catching the light, animated on twos with a gentle stop-motion jitter. Music: warm, about 92 bpm. One narrator voice.

## Three possible openings (the first 3 seconds; the rest of the ad is the same)
- Opening A (the one described above): on-screen "Sunday standoff."; Juno and a droopy fern face each other across the kitchen windowsill, frozen, like a western.
- Opening B: on-screen "How much is 'a bit'?"; Juno holds the watering can, squinting, the plant drooping beside her.
- Opening C: on-screen "Day 14. Still guessing."; Close on the fern by the window, Juno a blur in the background.

## The viewers in this feed
Only these groups of viewers react and post. The product, its brand, the character in the ad and its drawing style are parts of the ad, not people who post.
- Guilty plant owners: Adults in their 20s and 30s renting small flats with three to eight houseplants, who love them but have never fed them properly. Scroll fast in the evening; stop for anything that names a problem they feel guilty about, like a droopy plant. Doubt: does feed actually help, or is it light and watering?
- Plant hobbyists: Keen indoor gardeners with large collections who follow plant-care accounts and already use fertiliser. Stop for plant content but judge it hard; they look for the NPK numbers and dose. Doubt: why switch from what they already use, and what does it cost per litre?
- New plant parents: People who bought their first one or two plants this year, often as decor, and are anxious about killing them. Save and share simple how-to videos; a clear rule like 'one capful' appeals. Doubt: is this another thing they need to buy?
- Practical shoppers: Busy adults who buy houseplant supplies at the supermarket or online when something looks wrong. Skip most ads in under a second unless the product is obvious and the price feels fair. Doubt: what is it, where do they get it, and how much is it?
- Ad-weary scrollers: Viewers with no strong interest in plants who see the ad by chance and dislike sponsored posts. Swipe away from anything that looks like an ad; might stay only for a charming visual or a joke. Doubt: why is this in their feed at all?

## The question
A 20-second vertical paper cut-out video ad for Sprigly, a new liquid houseplant feed dosed with a measuring cap, appears between other short videos in the TikTok and Instagram Reels feeds of adults who keep houseplants, many watching with the sound off and captions on. Predict how these viewers react on social media: whether they stop scrolling in the first two seconds, whether they watch to the end, whether they remember the name Sprigly afterwards, whether they believe the simple claims (one capful per litre, every two weeks, with seaweed extract), whether they want to try or buy it, what they would comment or ask, and which of the three openings (A 'Sunday standoff.', B 'How much is a bit?', C 'Day 14. Still guessing.') would hold attention best.
