## The 12 strongest reusable rules (with evidence)
1. **Brand in the first second, story after.** 6/8 show or say the brand by 1.5 s; the story then runs with almost no brand mentions until the reveal. Only the creator-series episode waits (37 s) because the series itself is the hook.
2. **Reveal the thing being sold at ~60–85 % — unless it is a launch.** Parodies/stories: 62–84 %; launches/answers: 4.5–8 s (11–16 %).
3. **Borrow a format the viewer already knows and play it straight**: infomercial ("Ever try to…? But then… There's got to be a better way."), sincere anthem, sitcom + musical number, reality-show confessional, horror-game door check, food hack. 8/8 are parodies or native formats; 0/8 look like a classic spot.
4. **"But then…" escalation every 4–6 s.** Mountain water: 7 disasters in 45 s; same chain as `research/teardowns/` @1hidden_by_design's "If you… But… So…".
5. **Make the product the character's way to win a personal fight**, not a feature list: "I will not let [rival] have the satisfaction" (delivery app), a father's gift before a first date (lip balm).
6. **Prove a feature by plot instead of a demo**: the doorbell rings early → "there's no way it was that fast"; the bag is full of cans → "Honey, they're minis".
7. **Answer the audience on camera.** Stitching a customer's request and ending with "stop with the '…' comments, it's here!" earned 10.7 % likes and 3.7k comments (vs 0.5–2.5 % for produced spots).
8. **Mascot/characters as the threat or the joke get the highest engagement**: animated owl skit 12.6 % likes, 9.5M views in 25 s with 22 words.
9. **Switch format once at ~20 % and come back for the end** (sitcom → black-stage musical at 20.8 s → bedroom at 76 s) to reset attention inside 110 s.
10. **Put a twist after the product moment** so the ad doesn't end on the sell (cosmetics ad: family-reunion double twist at 81–98 s, after the reveal at 68 s).
11. **Write fine print as a joke in the brand voice**: "DO NOT BITE OFF LEG", "The suits want us to tell you to please don't actually send your pee."
12. **A series episode outperforms a branded segment.** The delivery-app episode of an existing series hit 6.9M with real comments; the brand-built reality segment with one person and no opponent got 0.98M boosted views and 23 comments. Add a second character with an opposing goal.

## Recurring phrasing
- "Ever try to [X] on your own?" · "But then…" · "There's got to be a better way." · "There it is." (infomercial chain)
- "I told you guys we were working on it. Here it is!" · "We thought about this!" · "So stop with the '…' comments." (reply format)
- "This is the only way you should be [using X]." · "You'll need: ✅ ✅ ✅" (hack format)
- "I'll show you my secret." · "Honey, they're minis." · "Preferably hydrated." (character sass)
- Slogan shape: "[Scary adjective] [place], [tasty adjective] [product]." ("Deadly mountains, delicious water.")

## Recurring visual styles
- **Cinematic comedy** (brand-funded): warm grades, practical gags, prosthetics/costume mascots, CG packshot only at the end.
- **Phone-native UGC**: selfie or tripod, real kitchens/cars, jump cuts, block captions or none.
- **Flat 2D mascot animation** with a genre overlay (CRT scanlines for a security cam), very few cuts, motion from poses.
- **Mascot costume shaped like the product** (a can-headed muscle suit) so wides carry the brand without a logo card.

## Notes for Ad Studio
- Direction stage: allow 0.8–9.8 cuts per 10 s depending on format (animated fixed-camera skits and food hacks are both valid); add a "borrowed format" field (infomercial, anthem, sitcom+song, reply-to-comment, genre parody, series episode) and a brand-map preset per format (launch: reveal ≤ 16 %; parody: reveal 60–85 %).
- Claims lint: the joke disclaimer pattern is compatible with "honest claims" as long as the joke does not state a false benefit.
