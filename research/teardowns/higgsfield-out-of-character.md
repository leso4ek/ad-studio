## Hook (0–3 s)
- First frame: Extreme low angle on churned mud and grass, a boot in the background, rain mist. Talent Contest watermark top right.
- First words: "A blueprint of fire, ultra-realistic, cinematic lighting, 4K, no subtitles." (a voice reads a prompt at 1.8 s)
- On-screen text: none
- Hook type: odd image + meta "prompt" line (the film is an AI actress being prompted)
- Open loop: Who is speaking a prompt over a battle? Then: a dragon (0:12) — is this a fantasy epic?
- First cut: 2.7 s

## Craft
- Cuts per 10 s: 2.43 (ffmpeg scene > 0.32; 105 cuts in 432 s, average shot 4.08 s, first cut 2.7 s)
- Shot types and camera: Wide establishing, then medium, then ECU on eyes; reveal shots low angle; dolly-ins on reaction close-ups; genre-matched lenses per set
- Captions: none burned in (dialogue film)
- Voice: Dialogue + VO, conversational pace
- Music / SFX: Score swells on the dragon; drops to silence for "Cut!"
- Visual style and consistency: Photoreal; consistency through one character element reused in every set and the same costume per set

## Payoff and end
- Payoff: Callback: the title phrase as the last line (404 s); the dragon becomes a pet
- End: Ends on a title card and fire; no CTA

## Why it works
- The first 15 s promise a fantasy epic, then the "Cut!" reverses it — a twist inside the first 40 s.
- A single recurring face carries 8+ genres; the consistency is the punchline.
- Meta humour about prompting makes AI the subject, not a weakness.

## What we would steal
- Open on spectacle, then reverse genre or context before 40 s (the "Cut!" move).
- Introduce the character with one line after the hook, not before.
- End on a callback of the title phrase.
