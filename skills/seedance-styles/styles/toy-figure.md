# Toy Figure (3D and stop-motion)
<!-- generated from studio/styles/catalog/toy-figure.json by studio/bin/catalog.mjs; edit the JSON -->

**Looks like:** compact collectible proportions: big head, short stubby limbs; moulded plastic with satin or gloss finish, seams and paint edges; painted or printed eyes; miniature dioramas shot like a product photo.
**Best for:** playful hero-product reveals, collectible or series ads with recurring characters, comedy where small figures take on a big problem. **Products:** small products that can sit at toy scale (bottles, snacks, gadgets, cosmetics); playful, collectible brands.

## STYLE block (paste verbatim into every shot prompt and every sheet prompt)
Collectible toy-figure look, photographed like a tabletop product shoot: characters are small moulded vinyl figures with compact proportions, a large head about half the body height, short stubby limbs and simplified mitten-like hands; satin plastic with crisp specular highlights, faint mould seams and tiny paint-application edges; eyes are painted, never wet; visible joints at neck, shoulders and hips; sets are miniature dioramas with slightly oversized props; macro-lens shallow depth of field, soft-box key light with a clean rim, everything at toy scale.

## Rendering
- Line: no outlines: crisp moulded edges and painted borders between colour areas
- Shading: glossy or satin plastic shading with sharp soft-box reflections and slight translucency at thin edges
- Colour: clean factory paint colours, each part one solid hue; product colours exact
- Texture: smooth plastic with mould seams, tiny paint overspill, a little dust at macro scale
- Lighting: large soft box key, white bounce card fill, hair-light rim; small tabletop scale

## MOTION block
Pick one mode per ad. Rigid toy stop-motion: figures repositioned on twos, bodies never bend, limbs rotate only at their joints. Or living toy: the same rigid plastic parts moving smoothly at the joints. In both, the plastic never deforms and faces stay as painted; expression changes happen on a cut.

## CAMERA habits
Macro lens 60-100 mm at tabletop height, slow slider moves, focus pulls between figure and product; the shallow focus is what sells the miniature scale.

## Avoid (name these instances only when the shot is at risk)
- plastic bending like rubber at elbows and knees
- skin pores on vinyl faces
- wet animated eyelids and eyes
- moulded logos or stamps on the base or back
- fingers sprouting from mitten hands

## Element sheets for consistency
Each figure as a boxed-collectible turnaround (front, three-quarter, side, back) on a seamless pale backdrop, with the joint positions and the head-to-body ratio described in the prompt and an unmarked base; the product as a toy-scale miniature beside the figure plus a straight-on packshot with the exact label.

## Where Seedance goes wrong in this style
Seedance wants to bring the toys to life: elbows bend, faces gain expressions and the plastic turns into skin. Choose rigid or living-toy timing and write it in MOTION; keep expression changes to cuts. It also invents moulded logos and copyright stamps on bases and backs; say 'unmarked base, no moulded lettering' in NO TEXT.
