# Paper Cut (Handcrafted illustration)

**Looks like:** layered cut shapes of coloured card stacked a few millimetres apart; exposed white or fibrous edges where the paper was cut or torn; small real cast shadows between layers; shallow, theatre-like depth.
**Best for:** simple product stories with clear shapes, nature, garden and seasonal scenes, explainers and step-by-step reveals, crafted, eco-minded brands. **Products:** eco, garden, food, stationery and gift products; friendly, natural, handmade brands.

## STYLE block (paste verbatim into every shot prompt and every sheet prompt)
Layered paper-cut animation shot on a tabletop: every character, prop and background is cut from matte coloured card and tissue, stacked in four to six layers a few millimetres apart; edges show the white core of the card or a soft torn fibre; each layer casts a small soft shadow on the one behind it; surfaces show paper grain and slight warping; characters are flat cut-outs with jointed limbs held by tiny pins; light comes from one warm lamp at the side, so depth reads only through shadows and focus, like a miniature paper theatre.

## Rendering
- Line: no drawn outlines: shapes are defined by cut edges, with a thin white or fibre edge showing the paper core
- Shading: only real cast shadows between layers and a soft falloff across a slightly curled sheet; no painted shading
- Colour: flat matte card colours, one colour per piece, a few patterned papers; the product cut in its exact colours
- Texture: paper grain, cut and torn edges, tiny wrinkles, slight warping, tissue translucency where light passes
- Lighting: one warm side lamp plus soft fill; shallow depth of field across the layers

## MOTION block
Stop-motion cut-out animation on twos: pieces move in small stepped jumps, rotating on their pin joints; the layers slide past each other on fixed planes, never bending in 3D; held poses are completely still; mouths and eyes are swapped paper pieces.

## CAMERA habits
Front-on rostrum or tabletop camera, locked or on a slow slider; focus pulls between layers; push-ins stop short of the paper surface; no camera turning around the set, because the flat layers would show.

## Avoid (name these instances only when the shot is at risk)
- smooth vector shapes with digital gradients
- characters turning in 3D like puppets
- paper texture swimming across moving pieces
- pieces bending like cloth
- printed letters on cut paper props

## Element sheets for consistency
Each character is shown as its set of cut pieces laid flat (head, body, limbs, two mouth shapes) and assembled front and profile; the place is shown as its layer stack from the front and at a slight angle so the gaps read; the product is a cut-paper version in its exact colours, with the label as plain shapes.

## Where Seedance goes wrong in this style
Seedance wants to give paper characters volume and turn them in 3D, and to let the paper grain slide across pieces as they move; repeat 'flat paper layers on fixed planes, rotate only on pin joints, stepped on twos' in MOTION and keep the camera frontal. Three-quarter turns are the weak spot: plan profile or front poses.
