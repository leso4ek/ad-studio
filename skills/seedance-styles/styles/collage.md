# Collage (Experimental and mixed media)

**Looks like:** cut-out photographs layered with flat coloured paper; overlapping images at mismatched scales; visible scissor edges, tape and glue shadows; mixed textures: newsprint grain, halftone, kraft paper.
**Best for:** energetic, rhythmic hooks, lifestyle and fashion stories, list or 'three reasons' structures, brands that want a zine or scrapbook voice. **Products:** fashion, beauty, music, snacks, lifestyle and youth products; bold, playful, creative brands.

## STYLE block (paste verbatim into every shot prompt and every sheet prompt)
Cut-and-paste collage animation: characters and props are cut from black-and-white and faded colour photographs and combined with flat shapes of coloured paper, kraft card and halftone printed scraps; pieces overlap at mismatched scales and slight angles, with white scissor borders, torn edges, strips of matte tape and small drop shadows where they lift off the board; textures mix newsprint grain, coarse halftone and paper fibre; a limited accent palette of one hot colour plus the paper tones; everything sits on a flat board lit evenly from above.

## Rendering
- Line: no drawn lines; shapes are defined by scissor cuts with a thin white border or a torn fibre edge
- Shading: photographic shading inside each cut-out, plus small drop shadows under lifted pieces; no global lighting
- Colour: desaturated photo pieces and paper neutrals with one hot accent colour; the product cut-out kept in its exact green and yellow as the brightest piece
- Texture: newsprint grain, halftone dots, kraft paper, tape, glue wrinkles, slight curling
- Lighting: flat even top light over the board; shadows only where pieces lift

## MOTION block
Stop-motion cut-out on twos and threes: pieces pop on and off in single frames, slide and rotate as flat cards, and replace each other in quick swaps; heads and hands are swapped cut-outs rather than animated; a few pieces tremble slightly between frames as if re-placed by hand.

## CAMERA habits
Top-down rostrum camera over the board; quick push-ins and snap zooms; pieces enter from off-frame; no 3D camera moves around the pieces.

## Avoid (name these instances only when the shot is at risk)
- seamless photoreal compositing with matched lighting
- pieces bending or turning in 3D
- a woman whose cut-out face changes between shots
- magazine headlines and printed words on the scraps
- smooth digital gradients

## Element sheets for consistency
The woman as one consistent set of photo cut-outs (head front, head three-quarter, body, two hand poses) with the same white border, so the face is reused, not re-invented; the place as a collage board with its paper pieces; the product as a clean cut-out of its exact bottle.

## Where Seedance goes wrong in this style
Seedance tends to blend the pieces into one photoreal composite and to fill newsprint scraps with garbled headlines; say 'separate flat cut-outs with white borders, no blending' and 'the newsprint is texture only, no readable words'. The photographed face is the identity risk; reuse the same cut-out head from the sheet and keep her face small or in profile.
