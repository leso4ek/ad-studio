# Viral playbook (from research/viral/, Nov 2026)

What the most-viewed TikToks of the last 12 months do, measured by watching them with the watch skill. The stage prompts embed the one section that matches the video's format (`playbook()` in `studio/server/stages.mjs`). Each section stays under about 1,600 characters. The evidence, counts and sources are in `research/viral/*-SUMMARY.md` and the teardowns.

## Explainers (2D and 3D)
- **Length:** about 60 s (median 62 s).
- **Pace:** 0.5–2.5 cuts per 10 s for animated or 3D work, and 3 words per second when narrated. 4 of 9 hits have no voice at all, only text.
- **Hook:**
  - make the premise about the viewer ("What if you…", POV hands, "your nose catches it");
  - or put a visible clock on frame 1 (a HUD countdown to the event, cut exactly at zero).
- **The chain:** re-hook every ≤ 10 s with "But…", a new countdown or a scale jump. Short pieces change the caption or claim every about 4 s.
- **The middle:**
  - one hard number at about 40 % of the runtime ("up to 50 %", "9 billion times a second");
  - close the premise and open a bigger one at 45–60 % ("But in five seconds… it all comes back").
- **Explaining:** use a scale ladder (one order of magnitude per beat) or a layer build (one part per problem), not cuts. Start with 2–5 s of the familiar real view, then x-ray it.
- **Charm:** give the mechanism a small personality ("tiny proteins trying their best").
- **Silent explainers:** a small-caps HUD for the numbers, plus one calm sentence of 8 words or fewer every about 4 s.
- **Ending:**
  - loop to frame 1, an open question, or "N seconds later";
  - CTAs only for sponsors: the sponsor answers the narrator's confusion, planted at about 40 %.
- **Phrasing:** "What if…", "But then…", "So the next time…", "It wasn't the X. It never is."
- **Sentence length:** 4–8 words for text, 8–14 when narrated.

## Skits (3D skit, sketch ads)
- **Pacing:** two presets.
  - **Scripted multi-role:** 5–7 cuts per 10 s.
  - **One-camera performance:** 0.6–2.5 cuts per 10 s.
  - The first cut lands at about 2.4 s.
- **Premise:** put a card at the top from frame 1 ("When your…:", "X be like:", "POV: …"), and hold it for 3–4 s or the whole video.
- **Material:**
  - retell something everyone knows (a ritual, a famous story, a format) and break it at every step;
  - or give the viewer a secret before the characters speak.
- **Escalation:** in threes, with each step delivered calmer. The strongest line lands at 55–70 %, followed by a short button of 3 s or less.
- **Ending:** the listener's reaction is the joke (a silent stare, a push-in), and the video ends on the punchline (6 of 8 hits).
- **Product skits:**
  - the product's own mechanic causes the turn;
  - its claim is spoken in a character's voice;
  - it is the only saturated colour in the frame;
  - the CTA is the final joke, said by a second character.
- **Dialogue:** 3–10 words per line; long run-ons only for the character who is the joke. Understatement and register clash ("humiliation ritual" for something trivial).
- **Visible clock:** a shrinking resource or time cards.

## Clips (stream and podcast)
- **Stream clips:**
  - about 18 s long;
  - start about 10 s before the moment and end 2–7 s after the reaction, cutting within 1 s once the reaction peaks;
  - a headline box states the payoff on frame 1 ("[Who] [VERB] after [what]" + 1–2 emoji) and holds for the first 9 s or the whole clip;
  - no speech captions on IRL clips (7 of 8);
  - a punch-in or reaction insert at the peak;
  - no freeze-frames, rewinds or zooms as hooks (0 of 8).
- **Podcast punchline clips:**
  - 25–40 s long;
  - start at the question that triggers the point, about 22 s before the punchline;
  - end 5–12 s after it on a button line;
  - 1–4-word captions that follow the speaker.
- **Podcast story clips:** 60–110 s, cold-opened on the most intense line, or built up to it as the last words.
- **Every clip:**
  - understandable alone by 3 s (or the headline carries the context);
  - starts at a sentence start, or a deliberate cold open;
  - never ends mid-conversation (the weakest hits did);
  - one moment, one emotion.
- **Publish threshold:** a rubric score of 70/100 or more, with no criterion at 0 (`research/viral/clip-rubric.md`).
