## The 12 strongest reusable rules (with evidence)
1. **Title card in frame 1, gone by 2–9 s.** 9/9 episodes; "Episode NN: Title" or the series name. It tells the feed this is a series without costing a shot.
2. **Never recap; re-hook in ≤ 3 s with "welcome back + tonight's event + one promise word", or with a new event.** 0/6 transitions used recap footage. AI host lines: "Welcome back to X. Tonight, Y. And trust me, things are about to get messy."
3. **Pay off the previous cliffhanger late, not first.** ep 2 paid off ep 1 at 80 % of runtime; s6e7 at 27 s; ep 22 in its last 12 s. Only a "she's coming over" cliffhanger is paid off at 0–2 s (s6e8).
4. **End within 4 s of the last line in live-action; within ~9 s in narrated formats.** Live-action 1.5–4 s; the AI host format uses 8.5–16 s for the teaser. Cut on the listener's face, not the speaker's.
5. **Alternate two storylines across consecutive episodes, and make each ending name the other.** AI eps 20/21/22 alternate boys/girls; ep 21 ends "we go back to the boys", ep 22 opens "We're heading back to the Boys' Casa".
6. **Identity without captions: colour-coded wardrobe + a name on a prop.** AI series: each character is one fruit/colour; live-action: a phone case printed with the character's name, one actor playing 6+ roles. 0/9 episodes use subtitles.
7. **One new face every 6–16 s in "arrival" episodes, each with a 3-part intro: name/age/origin → trait → threat.** ep 1 (9 characters in 70 s), ep 20 and 21 (4 each, every ~16 s), with a 2–3 s name super.
8. **Plant a spoken vow early and break it before the end.** ep 21 ("I plan on staying loyal" 47 s → "I'm lowkey into him" 129 s); ep 22 (the loyal one says "OK." at 164.6 s). Comments peak on these (60–63k).
9. **The peak line is the shortest line.** "OK." (ep 22), "Sorry, what?" (s6e6), "Where do you think?" (s6e7).
10. **Speed comes from dialogue when cuts are slow.** AI eps cut only 1.2–2.0×/10 s but keep 3.3–3.8 w/s ping-pong lines of 1–6 words; live-action runs 4.7–5.4 w/s.
11. **One held moment per episode.** s6e7: 10 s near-silence in a 4.4-cuts/10 s episode; s6e8: a 26 s unbroken rant take after 3 episodes of build-up.
12. **A third party carrying a secret is the most reliable cliffhanger engine.** "They told me not to say anything, but… [X] is talking about you." (s6e6) drove the next two episodes; the group chat itself (a message read aloud) is the inciting incident of 4/4 live-action episodes.

## Recurring phrasing
- Openers: "Welcome back to [show]." · "Tonight, [event]." · "And trust me, …" · "Wait, [name] wrote in the group chat." · "Oh my god, [name] just texted."
- Escalators: "Things get messy fast." · "Is she [expletive] serious?" · "It's like the calm before the storm." · "What the hell just happened?"
- Confrontation: "So I heard something you said about me." · "Keep my name out your mouth." · "Repeated back to you by who?" "It doesn't matter." · "So you hurt your own friend to achieve what?"
- Cliff lines: "This isn't over." · "Up next, a hot new [role] enters." · "Sorry, what?" · "Where do you think?" · "She's your problem now."
- Power words: messy, loyal, steal, secret, serious, trust, plot, villain, back ("I'm back").
