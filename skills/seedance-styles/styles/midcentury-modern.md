## STYLE block (paste verbatim into every shot prompt and every sheet prompt)
1950s-60s mid-century modern limited animation: characters are built from flat geometric shapes, triangles, trapezoids and ovals, with angular thin limbs, small dot eyes and sharp noses; colour is printed flat in a limited palette of mustard yellow, teal, coral, olive and warm grey on cream; outlines are thin, angular and slightly off-register from the colour fills; backgrounds are graphic and stylised, with flattened perspective, boomerang and starburst shapes, textured dry-brush and crayon patches; no gradients, no modelled shadows; the frame feels designed like a print layout.

## Rendering
- Line: thin, angular, slightly broken ink line drawn with a straight edge feel; lines sometimes drift off the colour shapes
- Shading: none modelled: a darker flat tone of the same hue as a geometric shadow shape, if anything
- Colour: limited mid-century palette (mustard, teal, coral, olive, warm grey, cream, black); each character two or three colours; the product colour exact and allowed to stand out
- Texture: dry-brush and crayon texture patches, faint paper tooth, slight printing irregularity
- Lighting: graphic rather than physical: day and night carried by palette swaps, not by cast light

## MOTION block
Limited animation on twos and threes: held poses with only the head, an arm or the mouth moving, sharp pose-to-pose snaps with little in-between, walk cycles on flat side-on planes, objects sliding in and out like cut shapes; graphic transitions where colour fields wipe across the frame.

## CAMERA habits
Flat, side-on and frontal staging, characters on a single plane; horizontal pans across long painted backgrounds, straight cuts, occasional snap zoom on a held drawing; no depth moves.

## Avoid (name these instances only when the shot is at risk)
- soft gradients and airbrushed shading
- rounded, puffy character shapes
- realistic perspective rooms with vanishing points
- smooth full-animation in-betweens
- invented lettering on posters or packaging

## Element sheets for consistency
Each character sheet shows front, three-quarter and side as flat geometric shapes in the exact palette on cream paper, plus a colour chip row; the product is redrawn as a flat geometric icon that keeps its exact proportions and colours; places are flat stylised layouts with a colour key.

## Where Seedance goes wrong in this style
Seedance pulls the flat graphic look toward softly shaded modern vector cartoons and invents real-room perspective in long clips; repeat 'flat colour, no gradients, flattened perspective' and keep moves to horizontal pans. Limited animation can read as frozen video, so name what moves in each time span.
