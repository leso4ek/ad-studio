# Mixed Media (Experimental and mixed media)
<!-- generated from studio/styles/catalog/mixed-media.json by studio/bin/catalog.mjs; edit the JSON -->

**Looks like:** a real photographed space with drawn and painted characters; ink line, acrylic paint and photography in one frame; digital marks (scribbles, highlights, arrows) drawn over the image; each medium keeping its own texture.
**Best for:** a drawn character in a real world, products shown as the one real thing in a drawn story, explainers that point at things, creative, design-led brands. **Products:** any physical product that should look real (bottles, cosmetics, gadgets); creative, modern, honest brands.

## STYLE block (paste verbatim into every shot prompt and every sheet prompt)
Mixed-media animation: the kitchen and the product are real photographs with natural daylight and true materials, while the woman and the plant are hand-drawn in a confident black ink line and filled with loose acrylic paint that leaves brush strokes and gaps; a few white and yellow digital marker marks (swooshes, small stars, a circle) are drawn over the photo to show energy and attention; the drawn parts cast soft painted shadows onto the real surfaces; each medium keeps its own texture, with photo grain, paint strokes and clean digital strokes, and never blends into one look.

## Rendering
- Line: bold black ink line on drawn characters; clean, single-weight digital marker strokes on top
- Shading: photographic light in the real plates; painted flat shadow tones on drawn figures; hand-painted contact shadows linking the two
- Colour: natural photo colour in the background; the drawn figures in a bright limited acrylic palette; the real product in its true colours
- Texture: photo grain, acrylic brush strokes with gaps, ink drag, crisp digital lines
- Lighting: real daylight in the photo; the drawn figures lit to match its direction with one painted shadow tone

## MOTION block
The photographed world moves in real time (light, steam, a smooth camera); the drawn character is animated on twos with a slight line boil; marker marks draw themselves on in a few frames and fade off; the two timings stay distinct, which is the point of the style.

## CAMERA habits
Real-camera moves in the photo world (a slow push-in, a gentle slide); drawn elements stick to the surfaces they touch; handheld only if the shot calls for it; no orbits that would show the drawings are flat.

## Avoid (name these instances only when the shot is at risk)
- the drawn woman turning into a photoreal person
- the real product redrawn as a cartoon
- everything melting into one painted filter
- digital marks that spell words or arrows labelled with text
- a different ink style in each shot

## Element sheets for consistency
Two kinds of element: the woman and the fern as drawn sheets (front, three-quarter, side) in the ink-and-acrylic look; the kitchen and the product as real photographs or photoreal renders. In every role line say which element is drawn and which is photographic.

## Where Seedance goes wrong in this style
Seedance's strongest pull is to unify the media: either the drawn woman becomes photoreal or the whole frame gets a painted filter. Give each reference a role line that names its medium ('drawn in ink and acrylic, keep drawn' / 'real photograph, keep photographic') and repeat it in CONTINUITY. Doodled marks can turn into text or numbers; describe them as shapes only.
