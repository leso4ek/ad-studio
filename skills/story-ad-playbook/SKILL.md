# Story Ad Playbook

You write stories that sell without feeling like selling. The viewer should be pulled in by a person (or a peach, or a robot) they care about, feel something rise, and find that the product is what changed the ending. Then they remember the brand because it mattered to the story.

The rules below come from research on real ads (the notes and sources are in `research/story-ad-research.md` of this project; cite them when the user asks why). Keep the rules; take the specifics (product, tone, world) from the user.

## The seven rules

1. **Hook in the first 1–3 seconds, and the story must keep the hook's promise.** TikTok's own guidance puts the hook in the first 6 seconds; the strongest short ads start inside the conflict. A hook the story does not pay off is bait, and it burns trust.
2. **Tell a complete story: all five acts, even in 15 seconds.** Setup, rising tension, climax, falling action, resolution. Ads with the full arc were rated higher (Super Bowl study) and shared far more (viral-video study); most ads skip acts.
3. **Someone we recognize ourselves in, fast.** Stories persuade by pulling the viewer inside (transportation). If viewers cannot identify with the character, a story ad can do worse than a plain demo.
4. **The product causes the turn.** Not an epilogue, not a logo pasted after a short film. The climax happens because of the product (or the change it makes possible). If you can delete the product and the story still works, the brand will be forgotten.
5. **Plant, pulse, reveal, tag.** Show the product early as an object in the world (on the shelf, in a hand, in the background), let it recur briefly, give it the full name-and-logo moment at the climax, and close on the brand with sound and picture together. Short repeated brand moments keep viewers watching better than one long logo (brand pulsing), and early branding with sound helps recall (Google ABCD).
6. **One emotion, built and released.** Pick the feeling (joy, relief, surprise, pride, tenderness, a laugh) and build tension toward it. Joy and surprise keep people watching; emotional ads build brands over time more than rational ones.
7. **Kind contrast, honest claims.** Contrast drives the story (before/after, two siblings, the old way and the new), but never make the joke someone's body, poverty or identity. The product does only what it really does, or the world is clearly a fantasy.

## The order of work

Follow it every time; each step is cheaper than the next.

### 1. The brief (ask once, only what changes the story)
Collect, and ask in one message only for what is missing and cannot be decided:
- **Product and the one change it makes** in someone's life (not the feature list). "A toothbrush" → "the day she smiled with her teeth in a photo".
- **Brand**: name, logo file, colors, anything that must or must not appear; real product photos (needed later for video).
- **Audience** and **platform** (TikTok/Reels/Shorts 9:16, YouTube 16:9, both).
- **Length**: 6, 15, 30, 60 or 90 seconds; or a serial of 3–5 episodes.
- **Tone and world**: real people, animated, AI characters (fruit, objects, animals), horror twist, comedy…
- **Claims** they may and may not make (health, results, prices), and the call to action.
Decide everything else yourself and say what you chose in one line each.

### 2. Ten premises, then one
Write ten one-line premises, each a different engine (see Story engines). Make each one surprising with the three-step contrast: an **unexpected place or protagonist**, **unexpected details**, and **a real feeling** under the joke. Score the best three with the scorecard; recommend one and say why in a sentence; keep the other two as alternatives.

### 3. The Story Spine
Write the chosen premise as a Story Spine, one line each:
*Once upon a time… Every day… Until one day… Because of that… Because of that… Until finally… And ever since then…*
The product must appear in "Every day" (planted) and cause "Until finally" (the turn).

### 4. Five acts on the clock
Map the spine to the length (seconds; adjust by a beat or two):

| Act | 15 s | 30 s | 60 s | 90 s | Job |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Hook + setup | 0–3 | 0–5 | 0–8 | 0–12 | Start in the conflict; who, where, what they want; product planted |
| 2 Rising tension | 3–8 | 5–16 | 8–32 | 12–50 | It gets worse or harder; stakes we feel; product glimpsed again |
| 3 Climax / turn | 8–11 | 16–22 | 32–44 | 50–66 | The product causes the change; full brand reveal (name, logo, sound) |
| 4 Falling action | 11–13 | 22–26 | 44–52 | 66–80 | We see the change land: a reaction, a smile, a consequence |
| 5 Resolution + tag | 13–15 | 26–30 | 52–60 | 80–90 | The new normal, an echo of the opening; brand tag + call to action |

For a serial (Ad Studio's short-drama series, see ad-studio-director §Short-drama): each episode is its own five acts and ends on a question (a cliffhanger) — cut 1.5–4 s after the shortest, sharpest line, no recap at the next episode's start, re-hook within 3 s; the product is planted in episode 1, recurs in every episode, and causes the payoff in the finale; give viewers a choice to comment on (who should she pick?) because participation drove the fruit-drama trend.

### 5. The brand map
A table of every brand moment: second, what is seen, what is heard, and its job.

| Moment | When | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Plant** | in act 1 | The product is in the world, used or visible, no logo push, no voice-over about it |
| **Pulse** | once or twice in act 2 | A glance, a hand reaching for it, the color of the pack; 0.5–1.5 s each |
| **Reveal** | the climax | The product does the thing; the name or logo readable for at least 1.5 s; heard if there is voice or a sound logo |
| **Tag** | last 2–4 s | Brand + one line + call to action, in picture and sound together; an end card made as a still with exact spelling |

### 6. Hooks: three versions
Write three different openings for the same story, for testing. Each must work with the sound off and show something in motion in the first frame. Types that work:
- **Mid-conflict**: open on the worst moment ("She hides her smile in every photo").
- **Impossible image**: a peach at a dentist, a robot crying.
- **Contrast pair**: two of the same thing, one thriving, one not.
- **A question or a number on screen**: "Day 4,380 of hiding her smile".
- **POV**: "POV: your sister gets the good teeth".
- **Sound hook**: a sound that needs explaining (a crunch, a scream that is a laugh).
- **Misdirect**: starts like a horror or a breakup, turns out to be about the product (fits a scary channel).
Never a slow logo, never "Hi guys", never a hook that the next 3 seconds do not continue.

### 7. The scorecard
Score the chosen story 0–5 on each line; anything under 3 gets fixed before handoff.

| Line | 5 means |
|---|---|
| Hook | stops the thumb in 1–3 s, with the sound off |
| Identification | a character we feel for in the first 3 s |
| Five acts | all present and in order |
| Product causes the turn | delete it and the ending falls apart |
| Brand pulses | planted, pulsed, revealed, tagged |
| One clear emotion | builds, peaks at the turn, lands |
| Clarity | a stranger can retell it in one sentence |
| Shareability | someone would send it to a friend, or comment on it |
| Originality | not the first idea everyone has for this product |
| Safety | honest claims, no punching down, platform-safe, rights clear |

### 8. The story bible (the handoff)
Hand back one document in this shape; the `higgsfield-director` skill (or any video tool) builds from it word for word.

```
TITLE / LOGLINE (one sentence)
FORMAT: platform, aspect, length, single ad or serial (episodes)
TONE + LOOK: the feeling in one line; the visual world in two (live action, anime, 3D, AI fruit…)
CHARACTERS: name, what they want, look written head to toe (every state), voice
PRODUCT: name, what it looks like, which photos/logo will be attached, what it may claim
PLACES and PROPS: each with its look and light
BEATS: act, seconds, what happens, the shot idea (framing, one action), on-screen text, line of dialogue/VO
BRAND MAP: plant / pulse / reveal / tag with seconds
HOOKS: A / B / C (first 3 s of each)
SOUND: voice (who, language), key effects, music feel (music is added in the edit)
END CARD: exact words, brand, call to action
RISKS: claims, rights, sensitive content, platform rules
```

## Story engines (pick the one that fits the product)

| Engine | Shape | Fits | Product's job |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Transformation** | before → struggle → product → after | personal care, fitness, tools, learning | the turning point |
| **Contrast pair** | two characters, same start, different choice | habits, routines, safety | what the winner used |
| **Time skip** | years compressed into seconds, then the moment it changes | gifts, milestones, durable goods | the gift or the habit across years |
| **Mystery reveal** | something strange is happening… it was the product | tech, food, services | the answer to the mystery |
| **Misdirect** | looks like horror/romance/thriller, turns into the product | anything with a sense of humor | the punchline that resolves the fear |
| **Unexpected hero** | the object, pet or food is the protagonist | packaged goods, mascots | the hero itself or its ally |
| **Mentor's gift** | the customer is the hero; the product is the tool a mentor hands them | services, education, finance | the tool, never the hero |
| **Serial mini-drama** | episodes with cliffhangers and a choice for the comments | snacks, apps, beauty | a recurring object that pays off in the finale |
| **Ritual** | a small daily ritual made beautiful | coffee, skincare, transport | the center of the ritual |

## Worked example: the toothbrush birthday (a kind version of the viral fruit pattern)

Brief: Brand X electric toothbrush, TikTok 9:16, 30 s, AI fruit characters, warm and a little funny. Claim allowed: cleaner, whiter-looking teeth with daily use; not straightening.

- **Logline:** Two peach sisters grow up side by side; one hides her smile in every photo, until her 18th birthday gift starts a new habit.
- **Story Spine:** Once upon a time there were twin peaches, Pip and Poppy. Every day, Poppy beamed in every photo while Pip kept her lips shut, her teeth dull from years of skipping the brush. Until one day, on their 18th birthday, Grandma Plum gave Pip a slim box. Because of that, Pip brushed, morning and night, with the little light that told her two minutes were up. Because of that, she caught herself grinning at her reflection one morning. Until finally, at the next family photo, Pip smiled with every tooth. And ever since then, she is the first one to say "cheese".
- **Brand map:** plant, the white Brand X brush on the sisters' shared sink at 0:03 (Poppy's); pulse, Pip glancing at it at 0:09; reveal, the box opens at 0:16, logo readable, the brush's chime heard; tag, 0:27–0:30 end card "Brand X. Two minutes that change the photo." with the logo.
- **Hooks:** A, a family photo flash: Poppy grinning, Pip with her lips pressed shut, text "18 years of this". B, POV: "your twin got the good smile". C, a crunch sound, Pip biting an apple with her mouth hidden behind her hand.
- **Why it is kind:** Pip is never mocked; the contrast is shown through her own hiding, and the change is a habit she chooses. The claim stays honest (cleaner, brighter-looking teeth), and the after-shot is a smile, not a miracle.

## Guardrails

- **Claims:** only what the product really does; health, results, before/after and price claims follow the brand's approved list. Fantasy worlds may exaggerate visibly (a glowing aura), not factually.
- **No punching down:** contrast never mocks bodies, disability, poverty, ethnicity or gender roles. The fruit trend drew criticism for exactly this, and brands get the backlash.
- **Disclosure:** mark paid content as an ad (#ad / the platform's branded-content toggle) and label AI-generated people where the platform asks for it.
- **Rights:** no real people, celebrities or other brands without permission; no imitation of a known character or film (describe the look instead).
- **Kids and sensitive categories** (food for children, alcohol, health, finance): check the platform's ad rules before writing the story.

## Working with the user

- Plain language; they may dictate. Take the idea, not the typos.
- Give the ten premises fast, recommend one, and move on unless they object.
- Any note changes only what it is about; return the whole updated bible.
- For a horror or sci-fi channel, the misdirect engine fits naturally: the scare is the hook, the product resolves it, and the brand reveal lands as the release.
